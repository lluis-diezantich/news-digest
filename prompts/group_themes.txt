You are given this week's candidate stories, each with an id, a headline and its
topics. Group the ones that belong to the SAME NARRATIVE.

A narrative is not an event. The stories in a group are usually DIFFERENT events
that a reader would want explained together because one thing connects them: the
same unfolding political episode, the same policy fight, the same conflict, or
the same mechanism showing up in more than one country. Clustering has already
merged the stories that are the same event, so none of these are duplicates.

Examples of a real group:
- an eviction, the protest it set off, the emergency measures it produced, and
  the investigation into who owns the building: one political episode.
- rent rises in one country, an election won on housing in another, and a
  campaigners' counter-summit: one mechanism in several places.

NOT a group:
- "everything about Europe", or every story sharing the topic `politics`. A
  shared topic is not a narrative. If the only thing two stories have in common
  is their topic word, leave them apart.
- two unrelated wars, however similar they look.

Rules:
- Every group needs at least 2 story ids. Do not invent a group to place a
  leftover story: a story that belongs to no narrative is left out entirely, and
  most weeks several will be.
- No story id appears in more than one group.
- At most $max_themes groups. Prefer fewer, larger, genuinely connected groups
  over many thin ones.
- Use only the ids you were given. Never invent an id.
- label: 3-8 words naming what connects them, in $output_language. Name the
  connection, not the topic -- "Spain's housing emergency becomes a European
  argument", not "Housing".

If the input carries `how_the_editors_described_their_week`, those are the
newsletters' OWN summaries of their issue, written by their editors. Treat them
as a hint about what mattered and nothing more: most sources do not publish one,
they describe only their own issue, and a group is still only real if the
headlines themselves support it. Never create a group that the story headlines do
not justify because an editor's blurb mentioned it.

Return the groups ordered with the most consequential first.
