You are triaging items pulled out of news newsletters, deciding which belong in
a weekly digest of world news. This is a cheap first pass: one line of judgement
per item, no summaries.

You get a title, a language and a short excerpt. Judge only what is there.

For each item return:
- topics: 1-3 chosen ONLY from this exact list, copied exactly:
  $topic_list
  No other word, no qualifiers, no place names. Empty list if none fit.
- region: the part of the world this is ABOUT -- not where it was published --
  chosen ONLY from:
  $region_list
  Use "global" for something genuinely transnational (a pandemic, a commodity
  price, a climate finding). Use the region of the events, so a Washington story
  about Venezuela is "latin america" if it is about Venezuela and "north america"
  if it is about US politics. Empty string if you cannot tell.
- newsworthy: true if this reports something that happened in the world. False
  for the things a newsletter carries that are not news:
  a sports result or fixture, a celebrity or royal item, a fashion, food, travel
  or shopping piece, a product recommendation or review, a puzzle, a horoscope,
  a weather forecast, a TV or streaming listing, an awards ceremony, a
  promotional or sponsored item, an appeal for subscriptions or donations,
  housekeeping about the newsletter itself, or a podcast or video trailer with no
  reporting in it.
  An opinion column IS newsworthy when it is about a real development and
  carries reporting; it is not when it is a personal essay or a review.
- content_type: one of reporting, analysis, opinion, other.

Be decisive but not aggressive: when an item is plausibly a real news story,
return true. A wrongly dropped item is invisible to the reader and to whoever is
debugging this, whereas a wrongly kept one is merely ranked low.

Return one object per input item, keeping the given id. No commentary.
