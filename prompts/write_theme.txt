Several related stories from this week's newsletters are given to you as one
theme, each with its headline, publisher and excerpt. Write the single digest
entry for the whole theme, in $output_language.

You are producing a news digest, not commentary. Summarize neutrally: distinguish
reported facts from claims, attribute disputed claims to whoever made them, avoid
persuasive or evaluative language, do not editorialize, and never rank or
characterize politicians or parties.

What makes this different from summarizing one story: your job is the CONNECTION.
A list of three summaries side by side is a failure. State what happened, then
make explicit what links the pieces -- the sequence of events, the shared cause,
the same pattern in two countries -- using only what the excerpts support.

- headline: neutral and factual, max 14 words, naming the theme rather than any
  one of its stories. Not a copy of any outlet's headline.
- narrative: 3-6 sentences. The events, in the order that makes them
  intelligible, with the connection between them stated rather than implied.
  Attribute each specific figure or finding to the outlet that reported it, by
  name, as the coverage list gives it. Do not pad to length: if the excerpts
  support three sentences, write three.
- why_it_matters: one sentence naming what is UNRESOLVED or now at stake -- the
  open question, the decision still to come. Not a restatement of the headline,
  not a forecast. EMPTY rather than padded.
- open_questions: up to 3 short questions this coverage does NOT answer, each one
  a thing a reader would reasonably want to know and the excerpts do not say.
  Leave EMPTY if the coverage is complete on its own terms. Never put a fact here
  as though it were a gap, and never invent a gap to fill the list.

Use only what the excerpts support. Do not add background you were not given. Do
not assert that two things are connected if the excerpts only place them in the
same week -- a coincidence of timing is not a narrative, and saying so is the one
error that would make this entry worse than publishing the stories separately.
